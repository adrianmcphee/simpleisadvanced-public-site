Reconcile the Company Continuously

The first synthesis run produces seventy-three discrepancies. The unit lead reads the output on a Tuesday morning, scrolling through a list that is longer than she expected and noisier than she hoped. Most of the flags are definitional: states named in the process definition that the code handles differently, transitions the code implements that the definition omits. She triages for forty minutes before she finds the first real one: the settlement calculation applies a discount threshold that the process definition doesn't mention, because the threshold was added eleven months ago by an engineer who updated the code but not the definition. The settlement has been silently discounting claims above a certain value. Nobody noticed because the output looked plausible.

The unit lead marks the flag, opens the process definition, and records the threshold as an undocumented deviation requiring review. It is the first time the artefact has forced a silent discrepancy into the open rather than quietly inheriting it.

Autonomous business units remove the need for coordination as governance, but not the need for shared understanding. AI systems can help by reading versioned artefacts repeatedly and producing synthesis on demand, reducing the dependence on heroic individuals to perform that reconciliation socially. This works only when the artefacts, the synthesis layer, and the controls connecting them are present.

The artefacts sit within a structural hierarchy, each level deriving from the one above it. The Strategic Context Stack connects enduring purpose and vision to the current mission, goals, falsifiable strategy, and funded bets. The process catalogue maps those active commitments to the organisation's value-creating processes and assigns them to owners. Autonomous units implement the processes they own. Each works within a bounded context that defines what data, services, and decisions belong to it. Operational data lives inside the boundary and serves the process, while contracts govern what crosses the boundary through versioned, machine-readable interfaces. The analytical domain sits downstream, consuming what units publish through data contracts rather than dictating what they store.

AI reconciliation reads the chain from strategic commitment through process definition to system telemetry and reports where it breaks. Whenever a level remains implicit, everything below it degrades into social negotiation and leaves the AI reader with nothing authoritative to reconcile against.

The artefacts that matter

Process definitions and contracts now sit in a hierarchy that gives the reader a traceable path from intent to outcome.

The vocabulary an organisation uses to govern itself is often unreliable. Purpose, vision, mission, strategy, goals: companies use these words differently, and often so loosely that they fail to constrain behaviour or connect a unit's work to stated ambition. A machine takes them literally, and direction-setting language that constrains nothing cannot be reconciled against the system.

The hierarchy is navigable because the artefacts reference each other. A current bet names the strategy and goal it tests, then identifies the processes expected to move. Work arising from an operating obligation names the law, control, incident, service promise, or contract that requires it. The catalogue names each process owner and its interactions; the definition records its states and transitions with the implementing systems; and each boundary points to a versioned contract. The formats are implementation choices, but the references must be stable enough that a reader can traverse them without reconstructing meaning from filenames and meeting notes or relying on institutional memory. The Technical Field Guide carries those references into delivery; a structured decision record completes the chain.

Change history is the new element in this hierarchy. Every material change to a process, contract, or system records its rationale and expected effect in a structured form. It does more than leave a terse changelog buried in a repository. A threshold change, for example, records both the expected increase in approvals and the maximum acceptable default rate, then adds the observed outcome when enough time has passed.

Structured change history turns the organisation into a learning system. An AI reader can trace which decisions produced their expected effects, which created unintended consequences, and which assumptions failed in a consistent direction. Causal judgement remains human, supported by an inspectable history more useful than another end-of-project “lessons learned” document no one reads.

Once these artefacts exist, AI also shifts from monitoring to exploration. A unit can model a change to a state transition, simulate the impact of a contract change on consumers, or find exception patterns that point beyond another workaround towards redesign. The analyst and domain expert can present an alternative against the process definition as evidence and avoid a meeting where the most fluent voice wins.

The continuous reader

The artefacts are valuable even on their own. They replace implicit knowledge with inspectable structure that makes onboarding easier and coordination cheaper while creating accountability. Their full value emerges when an AI system reads them continuously and synthesises them into a coherent picture of how the organisation behaves.

Concretely, the reader is narrower than the synthesis it builds towards. It takes a versioned process definition together with the code that implements the process and the telemetry the running system emits, and returns a Drift report. For example, the definition might say that every claim above the authority threshold must be escalated to a senior assessor, while the telemetry shows most of them clearing without one. The definition might list an exception path the code no longer contains. The form is always the same: definition says X, system does Y, with each gap made concrete in a named transition. What the reader cannot do is judge whether a gap, once flagged, was genuinely closed or merely defined away. A unit that relaxes its own definition to match what the code already does produces a clean reconciliation and has learned nothing. Telling real resolution from cosmetic redefinition means comparing outcomes over time, which remains a human judgement beyond the reader's remit.

The components of this synthesis are at different stages of maturity. The integrated picture, the full five-artefact hierarchy synthesised without human assembly, still requires custom engineering and is not yet available as a commodity product. Once assembled, it can do the following:

Weekly strategy reconciliation. The AI reads strategy commitments and process definitions against system telemetry and change history, then produces a brief synthesis of one to three pages that distinguishes commitments on track from those drifting or showing no recent activity, without diplomatic adjustment.

Contract violation detection. The AI monitors inter-unit contracts continuously. When a unit's behaviour deviates from its published contract, the violation is surfaced to the responsible and consuming units within the monitoring cadence. Resolution is tracked, and time to resolution becomes a unit-level metric in place of manual incident attribution.

Drift detection between stated process and observed behaviour. A continuous integrity check tells the unit when its description is becoming inaccurate through normal Drift and the process definition needs to evolve.

Decision-outcome tracing. This capability can narrow the gap between intent and result as it matures. The structured change history above is the prerequisite: without a versioned record connecting decisions to expected effects, there is nothing to trace. With it, the system can produce a bounded attribution hypothesis about an outcome and the decision that preceded it. Human validation of causal claims remains essential; the value of building the change history now is that the organisation accumulates the raw material needed to test those claims.

What the reader sees

WORKED EXAMPLE

This hypothetical trajectory shows how synthesis might improve as the artefacts it reads mature. The first cycle produces seventy-three discrepancies, mostly caused by artefact immaturity, with roughly one real signal for every five noise flags. Two later snapshots illustrate the intended economics.

By the third month, after a dozen cycles in which someone has triaged every flag and repaired the artefact behind it, the weekly output reports seven discrepancies. Three reflect genuine Drift: a timeout value changed in deployment but not in the definition, a new exception path added by an engineer who updated the code but not the definition, a contract SLA the adjacent team quietly relaxed. At this stage, the unit lead reviews the output in twenty minutes, leading to one definition update, one code fix, and one conversation with the adjacent team. The ratio has narrowed to three real signals to four noise flags, and the synthesis has become a diagnostic that prompts action rather than a report for passive reading.

By the ninth month the weekly synthesis is routine: two discrepancies, both caused by an adjacent team's contract change that shifted an input format. The unit lead uses the output to focus verification rather than accepting it as truth; each finding still requires provenance and a human disposition. The remaining conversation concerns the contract version, not whose narrative should prevail. The figures are a prediction to test. The expected gain is that review narrows as authoritative artefacts improve, while judgement never disappears.

The trajectory is the argument. The first cycle may be discouraging because it is noisy and effortful; later cycles should become useful enough to justify the discipline. Organisations that abandon the work after the first cycle never discover whether better artefacts and repeated triage would improve its economics.

When the team is one person

Drift has so far been framed as a coordination problem between people, but a prior version of it appears at the smallest scale. A single developer working with AI assistance can produce code at a rate previously achievable only by a team. What is missing is the shared context a colleague once supplied by saying, “that isn't how we agreed the state machine would work.” The old rule was that structure scales with team size; the AI-era rule is that it scales with rate of code production multiplied by Drift risk. High production can create in weeks a divergence between process intent and locally coherent code that once took a team months to accumulate. A process definition, contract tests, and automated Drift detection are the floor regardless of headcount, with production rate setting the synthesis cadence.

Fully autonomous agents extend the principle to its limit. An agent operating without a process definition has no mechanism to detect that its output has diverged from intent. Process definitions, contract tests, and bounded domain packages create the conditions under which agents operate productively. An agent's failure rate at a process boundary offers one measure of the coherence the organisation has built.

What the technology cannot do

The structural argument does not depend on specific tools, and these limitations will shift with the tooling.

A sceptical CTO making investment decisions still needs to know where the technology ends and human judgement begins.

Causal reasoning across organisational boundaries. Current systems can surface temporal associations: queue growth followed a pricing change, incident rates rose after a contract modification. They cannot reliably determine whether the first event caused the second, or whether both are effects of a third factor outside the evidence. Cross-unit degradation detection depends on a human reading the synthesis and applying domain judgement. Organisations that treat AI-generated causal claims as established fact will make expensive decisions on unsupported analysis.

Distinguishing intentional deviation from error. When a unit's process definition and system behaviour diverge, the reader cannot distinguish “the code is wrong” from “the definition is stale.” Human triage is required for every Drift flag. The burden scales with the number of units and the rate of change.

Confident synthesis of incomplete artefacts. A synthesis reporting “all commitments on track” may be wrong because the artefacts do not describe reality completely. A process definition that omits a manual workaround, a contract that specifies an SLA but not error semantics: each gap produces a confident assessment that misses a class of failure. AI synthesis is only as complete as its inputs, and material gaps may remain.

What makes synthesis trustworthy

The following operational controls are the minimum discipline that prevents the synthesis layer from reproducing the pathology it is meant to detect.

Closed-loop reconciliation. The synthesis scope must be bounded by artefacts: the system compares this definition against this code. Freeform queries against unstructured data produce an organisation that believes it has a reader when it has a writer, and the architecture must prevent this by design.

Provenance on every output. Every finding must trace to a specific artefact and code path within a named contract version and time window. “Retention is 73%” is not a reconciliation finding; “the customer_retention metric as defined in process definition v2.3, measured against production event logs from 2026-01-15 to 2026-02-14, shows 73.2% against a target of 80%” is, because only the second permits verification. If the AI cannot cite its source, the output is flagged as ungrounded, a design requirement for the pipeline.

Human triage as a permanent layer. The synthesis audits consistency, not truth: it can tell you the process definition and the code diverge, but not which one is right. That judgement belongs to the process owner, and the operational specification must define who reviews the output, on what cadence, how false positives feed back into the next cycle, and what happens when the reviewer disagrees. The triage burden decays but never reaches zero. Organisations that treat review as a temporary cost of early adoption will stop reviewing, and discover the consequences once a confidently wrong output has informed a decision.

Adversarial testing of synthesis outputs. Periodically feed the system known-bad inputs: a stale process definition, a deliberately contradictory contract, a manually overridden metric. Verify the reconciliation flags them; if it does not, the controls are failing silently. This is a fire drill for the pipeline, at a cadence matching risk tolerance, for example monthly for critical processes and quarterly for others. An organisation that has never tested whether its reader can detect a known divergence does not know whether it has a reader at all.

Closed-loop reconciliation, provenance, human triage, and adversarial testing together represent the minimum viable discipline for any organisation that intends to use AI to read its own artefacts and act on the output. The bounded capabilities are usable now; the integrated system is not a commodity product. As always, the question is whether the organisation is willing to do the structural work that makes those capabilities trustworthy without making them dangerous. AI synthesis is fallible: models hallucinate, and a synthesis reporting no divergences may be wrong, the wrongness arriving with the same calm authority as a correct finding. Explicit validation, checkable versioned artefacts, and human judgement applied to machine-generated evidence make a tireless and literal AI reader useful.

An AI synthesis model has its own versions of familiar pathologies. Gaming maintains artefacts to satisfy the reader without constraining behaviour. Drift can look like maintenance when the version increments while the description diverges from reality. A cold start requires diagnostic cycles before operational use. In every case, the defence is to audit runtime behaviour and customer outcomes, looking beyond artefact form.

Culture does not need to change in a separate programme before implementation begins. The first unit creates the practice: write one process precisely, reconcile it, accept correction, and repeat. The AI layer becomes useful as that discipline becomes ordinary. “The culture is not ready” is not a prerequisite; it is a result the first bounded implementation can test.

The shift in power

When synthesis becomes continuous, stewardship gains authority while position alone carries less. The person who maintains an important process definition can shape operating reality because the definition constrains decisions in ways a title does not. The synthesis layer makes visible a redistribution already created by explicit ownership, making the alternative harder to sustain.

The operating state

The result should be observable and specific. Strategy names commitments that map to owned processes. Process definitions constrain operation. Contracts make cross-unit promises testable. Change records state expected effects. Production evidence tests whether those effects occurred. Discrepancies receive a human disposition, and governance can inspect the chain without taking over the process.

The company still needs judgement, argument, and occasional coordination. It no longer needs recurring alignment to manufacture a temporary version of shared reality. The conclusion returns to the human and governing choices that determine whether any of this will be used. The technical field guide that follows it preserves the exact last-mile protocol for implementers.
