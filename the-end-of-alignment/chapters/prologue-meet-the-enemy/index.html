Prologue: Meet the Enemy

Every bad operating model eventually creates an industry to protect it.

The Alignment-Industrial Complex is the system of recurring meetings, translation roles, governance frameworks, offsites, steering groups, programmes, and management rituals that grows when no one owns a complete outcome. It turns missing ownership into permanent work. Intelligent people keep incompatible parts of the company moving by negotiating across boundaries the company refuses to repair. Vendors sell methods for making the negotiation more mature.

That system is the enemy in this book, not the consultant, programme leader, architect, product manager, Scrum Master, or executive working inside it. The participants are often competent and behaving rationally. Their competence is what allows the structure to survive.

Standing alignment substitutes recurring coordination for ownership. It consumes the time in which a decision still matters, distributes accountability across everyone in the room, and leaves the operating mechanism unchanged. One function holds the promise, another the process, another the system, another the data, and another the report. The company can govern the represented version because reconciling it with the running version has been slower and more political than polishing the representation.

That economic assumption is changing. AI is usually introduced as a faster writer: more code, requirements, summaries, policies, and presentations. Used that way inside an incoherent company, it accelerates the manufacture of claims. Its more consequential use is as a reader: tracing intent through process, contracts, code, deployment, operation, economics, and customer outcome. The writer makes the narrative more fluent. The reader asks whether the evidence supports it.

The correction is not another alignment method. It is to design the company so that important state has an owner, boundaries contain useful outcomes, interfaces are explicit, and evidence can travel from promise to production without being reconstructed in a meeting. The next chapter begins with the industry that forms when a company declines to do that.
