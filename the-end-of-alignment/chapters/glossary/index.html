Glossary

Autonomous business unit. The smallest durable organisational structure accountable for a complete business process and outcome. It owns the process, capability composition, software, operational data, contracts, delivery, first-line risk, economics, and performance inside explicit institutional constraints.

Candidate. One exact proposed source state and its generated artefacts presented for review or integration. If reconciliation changes the content or meaning, it becomes a new candidate and the required proof repeats.

Contract. A versioned, testable statement of meaning and behaviour between two units or between a unit and a supplier. It includes obligations, compatibility, failure behaviour, evidence, and change rules; it is more than a schema or document.

Controlled exposure. The deliberate act of making a deployed behaviour available to a defined customer, operator, cohort, or market. Integration, deployment, and exposure are separate facts.

Drive. What became newly true and materially advanced the current milestone. Output volume and completed tasks do not qualify unless they change the promised outcome.

Drift. Deviation introduced while moving towards a milestone, including inconsistency in experience, domain language, architecture, contracts, verification, or production behaviour. Net progress is Drive reduced by the Drift that must be repaid.

Lane. Temporary authority to carry one independently useful vertical slice from accepted intent to verified integration. A lane has one accountable writing owner, exact leases, evidence, stop conditions, and a retirement rule.

Lease. Bounded permission for one holder to mutate an exact physical or semantic resource. Leases cover files when files are the shared state, but may also cover schemas, contracts, generators, feature-flag namespaces, environments, credentials, measurement hosts, or production operations.

Milestone. A stable, observable outcome that should become true next. It states the customer or operator result, governing flows, important language, boundaries, exclusions, and required proof without pretending to know every task in advance.

Orchestrator. The traffic controller for temporary execution authority. It admits and sequences ready work, protects leases and scarce resources, tracks candidate identity and retirement, and routes decisions to their owners. It does not own the product, process, risk appetite, or quality bar.

Process definition. A versioned account of the states, transitions, decisions, invariants, failure modes, evidence, and contracts through which a business process fulfils its promise. Runtime behaviour must be able to prove it wrong.

Process catalogue. The operating org chart of what the company does: its material value-producing processes, outcomes, owners, promises, and interactions. It makes strategic commitments assignable without treating the reporting hierarchy as the operating mechanism.

Product authority. The authority that owns a customer promise, vision, economics, quality bar, and ordered milestone sequence. It is distinct from a portfolio label and need not own every process that fulfils the promise.

Proof Synthesis. Continuous reconciliation of the current milestone, product, process, source, production state, and evidence. It shows what is proven, partly evidenced, open, blocked, or drifting and proposes candidate next moves without authorising them all.

Reconciliation. A bounded comparison between a declared artefact and the mechanism or evidence it describes. It detects consistency and divergence; it does not decide which meaning ought to prevail or establish causality by itself.

Represented company. The organisation described by strategy, portfolio, architecture, process, financial, and governance artefacts.

Running company. The organisation expressed by real decisions, software, operational work, suppliers, data flows, deployments, controls, and customer outcomes.

Slice. The smallest independently useful movement of a complete outcome through production behaviour. A slice is vertical because it closes the relevant path from intent to observed consequence rather than completing one technical layer.

Software-dependent corporate. A company whose products or operations depend critically on software while its authority model still treats software and engineering as support functions outside “the business.”

Strategic Context Stack. The linked hierarchy of purpose, vision, mission, goals, strategy, bets, OKRs, and KPIs. Each layer has a distinct horizon, authority, revision rule, and relationship to the layers above and below it, allowing current work to trace to durable direction without recurring social reconstruction.

Trunk. The single shared integration truth in source control. Frequent integration keeps assumptions current; it does not imply that every integrated behaviour is deployed or exposed.

Worktree. An isolated checkout of source used to prepare a candidate without colliding with another checkout. It isolates mutable files but grants no authority to change them.
