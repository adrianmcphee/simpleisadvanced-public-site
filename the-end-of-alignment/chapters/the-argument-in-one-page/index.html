The Argument in One Page

Software-dependent corporates place engineering outside “the business” while depending on software to operate. The resulting split separates authority over a process from authority over the mechanism that performs it. Product roles, programmes, committees, OKRs, shared platforms, and alignment rituals then carry context across the boundary. Capable people keep the structure survivable; their work can also prevent its cause from becoming unavoidable.

The correction begins with a complete business process and outcome. One accountable unit joins the operational, commercial, engineering, data, control, and supplier capability required to perform it. The unit owns its process and operational state, publishes explicit contracts at genuine boundaries, and acts inside institutional constraints that governance can inspect and enforce without taking over ordinary operation.

Architecture follows the same decomposition. When a company has no shared account of its processes, engineers infer boundaries from departments, applications, vendors, projects, and databases. The real process crosses those proxies, so ordinary change crosses services and authorities. Organisational ambiguity becomes technical coupling.

AI changes the economics on both sides. It makes fragmented context more expensive because execution can outrun coordination. It makes explicit operating logic more valuable because versioned direction, process definitions, decisions, contracts, and evidence can guide people and agents repeatedly.
