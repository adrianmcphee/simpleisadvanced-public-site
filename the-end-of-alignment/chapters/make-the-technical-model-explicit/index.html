Make the Technical Model Explicit

An operating model that stops at boxes and accountabilities is management theatre. In a software-dependent corporate, authority becomes real in source control, contracts, tests, deployment, configuration, credentials, and production. If the model cannot explain how a milestone becomes customer-visible behaviour without losing ownership or evidence, it has avoided the place where the company actually operates.

The board does not need to administer Git. It does need to know whether the delivery system preserves the authority the board believes it delegated. The CTO needs the complete mechanics. They appear in the Technical Field Guide at the back of this book.

One chain from capital to consequence

The chain begins with the current strategic context and the portfolio of product theses and customer promises it permits. Portfolio authority then decides which bets deserve capital. Product authority protects the coherence of each promise. Process-owning units maintain the capabilities that fulfil it. A unit may also have to act because an explicit operating obligation has arisen: an incident, control breach, regulatory change, service-health failure, or contractual duty. Each unit works through an ordered roadmap of complete outcomes, with one current milestone stable enough to steer against and evidence that changes as the system changes.

The complete chain is simple to state:

Strategic context $$ product thesis and promise $$ funded bet or operating obligation $$ process owner $$ unit roadmap $$ current milestone $$ vertical slice $$ named lane and leases $$ source candidate $$ trunk $$ deployment $$ controlled exposure $$ outcome evidence.

Every arrow is a change of authority or state. A product promise is not permission to edit arbitrary source. A green source candidate is not yet integrated truth. Integrated source is not necessarily deployed. A deployed feature is not necessarily exposed. Customer exposure is not evidence that the promised outcome occurred. When companies blur these states, nobody can say which version was tested, what is serving, who can experience it, or whether it worked.

Product truth advances only as far as the evidence. That is the technical expression of honest governance.

Durable ownership, temporary authority

The autonomous unit owns the process over time. A delivery lane receives temporary authority to carry one independently useful slice, and leases bind that authority to the physical and semantic resources the slice can affect. A worktree isolates files; a branch records lineage. Neither grants permission. An agent may be technically able to change the whole monorepo while being authorised to change only the resources named by its leases.

The same separation applies to state. Integrated source, a deployed artefact, customer exposure, and an observed outcome are different facts. A board does not need the commands used to move among them. It needs confidence that each fact has an owner, an exact identity, evidence, and a retirement rule for temporary authority.

Agentic execution makes this discipline more important because starting work is cheap while integrating meaning remains expensive. The orchestrator preserves sequence and resource safety inside the authority already granted. It cannot redefine the product, lower the quality bar, accept risk, or become a permanent approval layer.

The governing invariants

Executives do not need every command in the protocol. They should insist on eight facts.

  • Every current slice traces to an active bet or explicit operating obligation, an accepted product promise or process duty, a process owner, and a milestone.
  • Every writing actor belongs to one named lane with one accountable writing owner.
  • Every mutation has an active lease over the physical and semantic resources it can affect.
  • Every reviewed candidate has one immutable identity; a reconciliation that changes it creates a new candidate and repeats the required review.
  • Trunk integration, deployment, and customer exposure are recorded as separate facts.
  • Production claims bind to the exact source, artefact, configuration, audience, and observation window.
  • Product and process truth never advance beyond the evidence.
  • A lane is not complete until branches, worktrees, leases, temporary flags, migration versions, and recovery state have retired or received an explicit continuing owner.

These rules create operational evidence. They let a company answer after an incident, a regulatory question, or an agent failure: who was authorised, what changed, which exact thing ran, who experienced it, what was observed, and what temporary authority still exists.

How a director reads the chain

WORKED EXAMPLE: A policy-renewal change

Assume a renewal unit wants customers to change a low-risk policy detail without entering a manual queue. The product authority protects the promise that an eligible customer can complete the change in one visit. The process owner defines eligibility, the manual exceptions, the control evidence, and the outcome to observe. The portfolio decision funds that promise. Only then does a delivery lane receive temporary authority to change the relevant process and system.

The board pack does not need a branch history. It needs a short set of claims tied to mutually consistent identities:

  • the accepted promise, process owner, current milestone, and constraints;
  • the lane carrying the change and the resources leased to it;
  • the immutable source candidate that passed review and the later trunk identity into which it was integrated;
  • the artefact and configuration deployed to the production environment;
  • the audience for whom the change was exposed and the observation window used to judge it;
  • the outcome evidence, remaining Drift, and temporary state still awaiting retirement.

At the first review, the source may be integrated and the artefact present in production while customer exposure remains disabled. A conventional status report can call that state delivered because the project team has finished its work. The explicit chain says something narrower and more useful: the production mechanism is ready, no customer has received the promise, and no outcome claim has yet been earned. The director can ask why exposure remains disabled and who owns the answer. A named legal, operational, or risk threshold may justify the hold. An unnamed dependency reveals a boundary the operating model still needs to repair.

At the next review, the exact artefact may be exposed to a declared audience. Production events then show which eligible renewals completed the path, which entered an exception, whether the control operated, and whether another customer outcome deteriorated. The unit compares that evidence with the milestone and records a human judgement. A mismatch can trigger repair, rollback, or a properly authorised revision of the promise. Evidence that supports the milestone advances product and process truth.

Completion also has a visible meaning. The lane closes, its leases are released, the worktree and branch retire, and temporary flags or migration versions disappear or acquire an explicit continuing owner. Forgotten temporary authority is therefore inspectable before it becomes part of the permanent system.

For a director, the technical model has one landing point: every material statement in the board pack should bind to one exact state in the delivery chain. The CTO and unit lead need the machinery underneath. The board needs representations that cannot silently merge accepted intent, reviewed source, deployed software, customer exposure, and observed consequence into one reassuring word such as “done.”

The Technical Field Guide specifies the mechanics once: names, lanes, path leases, semantic leases, worktrees, trunk integration, contracts, feature flags, source and deployment identities, recovery, the monorepo topology, and the complete execution state machine. Its purpose is to remove the recurring meetings that appear when the company cannot trust its own boundaries or state.

The technical model is explicit because work ships through it. Anything less leaves the operating model above the machinery through which the business changes, and recreates the separation this book is meant to end.
