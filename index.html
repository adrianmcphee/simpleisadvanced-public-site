Is this about your company?

If your company uses the business to mean a group of people that does not include engineers, and the company depends on software to sell, serve customers, manage risk or run operations, it is a software-dependent corporate. Its organisation chart says technology is a support function. Its operating reality says otherwise.

These companies separated operations, product, engineering, data, risk and finance, then built an alignment economy to stitch them back together. Meetings multiply. Translation roles appear between people who should share one context. Portfolio labels describe products that nobody can find in the code. Large programmes move slowly while every participant reports progress.

AI makes that arrangement less defensible. Competitors that put engineers inside the operating context can read evidence faster, change processes faster and learn faster. A company that still waits for alignment meetings cannot compete with one that has made authority, boundaries and evidence explicit.

What the book develops

One shared context

A clear line from purpose and strategy to milestones, business processes, customer flows, system boundaries, measures and production evidence.

Real operating authority

Autonomous units that own complete processes, their technology, staffing, agents, suppliers, economics and risks, while working through explicit contracts with other units.

A technical method that ships

Trunk-based development, delivery lanes, short leases, worktrees, feature flags and continuous reconciliation so many teams and agents can change one platform without creating parallel companies.

The argument in one line

Agility and speed are strategy. They come from structural clarity and accountable ownership, not from more ceremonies, more coordination or a better alignment narrative.

The End of Alignment is a short, practical book about the logic required to realise an AI-era operating model. It is not a catalogue of roles or rituals. It is an argument for integrating operations and technology around the processes through which value, cost and risk actually move.
